================================================================================
BOT BATCH TRACKER -- EVERY FILTER IN THE PIPELINE, IN PLAIN ENGLISH
Written 2026-08-11. Defaults are what a run uses if nobody touches a slider.
================================================================================

HOW TO READ THIS
----------------
A "filter" here means anything that decides a point, a frame, or a whole track
does not go into the export. Some of them do not delete -- they mark a frame as
occluded, or join two tracks, or add tracks back. Those are called out.

The bot has two tracking backends:

  * SYNTHEYES  -- the default. It has its own tracker, so it gets only the mask
                  post-filter (section G). Everything else below does not run.
  * TAPNEXT++  -- the GPU fallback / comparison engine. It runs the whole stack,
                  filters 1 through 42.

Order matters. The list is in the order a track actually meets them.

Legend:  [ON]  = active with default settings
         [OFF] = present, wired, but switched off by default
         [COND]= only fires when a condition is met


================================================================================
SECTION A -- SEEDING: choosing which pixels to track at all
(app/tracker_core.py, _detect_features / _staggered_queries / _drop_edge_points)
================================================================================

1. CORNER STRENGTH BAR (feature_quality = 0.02)                            [ON]
   Before tracking anything, the bot looks at a frame and finds the corners.
   OpenCV scores every candidate and this throws away anything scoring below 2%
   of the best corner in that frame. It is a RELATIVE bar, so on a plate with
   one brilliant corner and a lot of mush, the mush goes.
   The auto-tune moves it: 0.015 on a detailed plate (let more in), 0.06 on a
   low-texture plate (2% of the best corner there is just noise).

2. MINIMUM SEED SPACING (min_feature_dist = 12 px)                         [ON]
   No two seeds may start closer than 12 pixels. Stops fifty points piling onto
   one high-contrast bolt while the rest of the frame gets nothing.
   Auto-tune raises it to 16 on fast/handheld shots and 18 on low-texture ones.

3. SAM3 SEED REGION (mask_mode = "outside")                        [ON w/ masks]
   The mask stage has already painted where the moving characters and props are.
   In "outside" mode the bot is only allowed to plant seeds OFF those movers --
   because a camera track wants the static world, not the actor. In "inside"
   mode the rule flips (used when tracking an object rather than the camera).

4. MASK EDGE PULL-IN AT SEED TIME (mask_margin_px = 8 px)                  [ON]
   SAM3 mattes usually stop a few pixels short of the real silhouette -- hair,
   motion-blur fringe. A point seeded in that thin halo is actually stuck to the
   character and slides with them. So the keep-region is shrunk 8 px inward
   before any seed is planted.

5. APERTURE / EDGE REJECT (min_corner_anisotropy = 0.08)                   [ON]
   A point sitting on a plain line -- a rope, a table edge, the border of a TV
   screen -- can be located ACROSS the line but not ALONG it. It will slide up
   and down that line all shot and look fine while doing it. The bot measures
   how "corner-like" versus "line-like" each seed is (1.0 = perfect corner,
   0.0 = pure line) and drops anything under 0.08. This test does not care about
   contrast, so unlike filter 1 it does not also throw away faint real corners.

5b. ADAPTIVE EDGE FLOOR (adaptive_anisotropy)                             [OFF]
   Optional upgrade to filter 5: instead of the fixed 0.08, look at THIS frame's
   own spread of corner-vs-edge scores, and if it clearly splits into two groups,
   cut at the split. Refuses to fire if it would remove more than half the seeds
   (a plate made entirely of edges has nothing better to offer). Off by default.

6. ALREADY-CLAIMED GROUND (claim radius = min_feature_dist)                [ON]
   Seeds are planted at 4 different moments inside each window, not all at frame
   one. Without this filter each of those 4 rounds would re-detect the SAME
   corners (the frames barely differ) and produce 4 duplicate tracks per point.
   So each round paints over the ground it took, and later rounds may only seed
   what is genuinely new -- content that just entered or was revealed.

7. PER-WINDOW SEED BUDGET (60 seeds per megapixel, floor 64, ceiling 1200) [ON]
   Re-seeding runs every 30 frames. Without a cap, a 300-frame shot would ask
   for the full seed count ten times over and saturate the machine. Instead each
   window gets a budget scaled to the frame's resolution, so the total across a
   shot grows with its LENGTH and resolution instead of exploding.


================================================================================
SECTION B -- MASK GATING: what the movers cover
(app/tracker_core.py, _apply_per_frame_mask_gating)
================================================================================

8. PER-FRAME OCCLUSION MARKING (occlusion_continuity)                      [ON]
   NOT A DELETE. When a character walks in front of a tracked point, those
   frames are marked occluded and the track carries a HOLE through them. It used
   to delete the whole track for the whole shot because of one crossing.
   3DE accepts gapped tracks natively, so a hole is a perfectly good answer.

9. OCCLUSION FLICKER GUARD (min_occlusion_run = 3 frames)                  [ON]
   A point sitting right on the mask boundary flips in and out from sub-pixel
   jitter alone, so the track appears to blink on and off while its pattern is
   plainly visible. A real crossing lasts. Occluded runs shorter than 3 frames
   are treated as boundary chatter and those frames are put back.

10. INSIDE-MODE RESIDENCY (inside_ratio = 0.80)                          [COND]
   Only used when mask_mode is "inside" (object tracking). A track must sit
   inside the object mask for at least 80% of its life or it is dropped whole --
   an object track that wanders off its object is not an object track.

11. MASK EDGE PULL-IN AT GATE TIME (mask_margin_px = 8 px)                 [ON]
   The same 8 px margin as filter 4, but applied every frame while gating. This
   catches a track that started on safe ground and later DRIFTED into the halo
   around a character -- something a seed-time-only check would miss.


================================================================================
SECTION C -- MOTION / SANITY FILTER (per pass)
(app/tracker_core.py, _post_filter_tracks and _bad_track)
================================================================================

12. VISIBILITY RATIO (min_visibility_ratio = 0.05)                         [ON]
   A track must be visible for at least 5% of the frames it spans. Anything
   below that never really existed.

13. MOTION-INLIER RATIO (min_motion_inlier_ratio = 0.10, residual 1% of diag)
                                                                          [ON]
   For each frame the bot works out the MEDIAN movement of all points -- roughly
   "what the camera did". A track must move in rough agreement with that on at
   least 10% of its frames. This is deliberately a very loose bar: a point with
   real parallax (a close foreground object) genuinely does not match the median,
   and must not be punished for it. This only catches total nonsense.

14. MAXIMUM JUMP (max_jump_diag_frac = 0.05 of the frame diagonal)         [ON]
   If a track ever teleports more than 5% of the frame diagonal in one frame,
   it was never tracking a thing. Dropped.

15. NaN / INFINITY GUARD                                                   [ON]
   Any sample the tracker leaked as not-a-number or infinity is marked invisible
   before anything else looks at it. Purely defensive.

16. MINIMUM SURVIVING POINTS (min_track_points = 8)                        [ON]
   After occlusion holes and gating have taken their bites, a track with fewer
   than 8 usable frames left is not worth carrying forward.

17. JUMP / JITTER REJECT (filter_max_jump_px, filter_max_jitter_px)       [OFF]
   Optional hard limits in plate pixels: drop a track if any single-frame step
   exceeds X, or if its average change-in-speed exceeds Y. Both default to 0
   (off) because they are blunt -- a fast but SMOOTH high-parallax point passes,
   but tuning them per shot is guesswork. Left as sliders.

18. PATH SMOOTHING (smooth_window = 0)                                    [OFF]
   Optional moving-average over each track's path. Off, because smoothing a
   track hides tracking error rather than fixing it, and 3DE would rather have
   the truth.


================================================================================
SECTION D -- CANDIDATE STAGE: choosing which tracks ship
(app/track_filter.py)
================================================================================

19. PASS STITCHING (stitch_passes: 1.5 px apart, 4 frames overlap, 2 frame gap)
                                                                          [ON]
   NOT A DELETE -- A REPAIR. The bot runs 4 tracking passes (forwards, backwards,
   from the middle) and seeds enter at staggered times, so one pass may hold the
   first half of a feature's life and another the second half, under two
   different IDs. Two features never sit 1.5 px apart for 4 straight frames, so
   that pairing is trusted and the two halves are joined into one long track --
   BEFORE anything judges tracks on length. Without this, the length gate saw
   two short tracks and the spacing gate then deleted one as a duplicate.

20. PASS FUSION (fuse_passes)                                             [OFF]
   Would AVERAGE the passes that agree instead of picking one. The arithmetic
   is correct and it beats an unweighted mean on constructed tests -- but it was
   measured on two real bench shots and did not help: no gain at the median, and
   the WORST track got worse on both. Reason: it runs before the refine stage,
   so it averages two coarse guesses and can hand the refiner a start point
   sitting between two features rather than on either. Left off with the numbers
   recorded in the code so it is not silently re-enabled.

21. LENGTH GATE (min_track_frames = 24)                                    [ON]
   A track shorter than 24 frames does not constrain a camera solve; it just
   makes the artist scroll. Scaled down on short shots -- it will never demand
   more than a quarter of the shot's length, so a 40-frame plate is not gated
   into nothing.

21b. SPAN-FRACTION GATE (min_track_span_frac = 0.0)                       [OFF]
   Same idea as 21 but expressed as a fraction of the shot. Off; the absolute
   frame count is used instead.

21c. QUALITY SCORE FLOOR (min_track_score = 0.35)                          [ON]
   Every track gets a 0-1 score built from three things it can be judged on
   WITHOUT comparing it to other tracks (so parallax is never punished):
     - coverage  (50%): how much of the shot it covers, how few holes it has
     - smoothness(30%): how little its own path jitters
     - stability (20%): how small its worst single-frame jump is
   Below 0.35 it does not ship. Note this score is a good ranking tool and a
   POOR error predictor -- it rewards long unbroken tracks, and a smooth drifter
   is exactly that. Filters 37 and 40 exist because of that blind spot.

21d. GATE RELAXATION FLOOR (quality_gate_floor = 8)                        [ON]
   Safety rail on 21 and 21c. If the gates would leave fewer than 8 tracks, they
   are relaxed and the best 8 are kept instead. A hard shot returning something
   flagged beats a hard shot returning nothing.

22. EVEN SPREAD SELECTION (track_spacing_px = 60 px quoted at 1920 wide)   [ON]
   The 4 passes produce up to 4 copies of every physical point, plus natural
   clumping on high-contrast areas. This walks the list best-score-first and
   accepts a track only if it stays at least 60 px from every track already
   accepted -- giving an even blanket over the frame instead of clusters.
   Two important details:
     - spacing is measured ON SCREEN at 5 evenly-spaced frames, not on each
       track's average position. With a moving camera two tracks can sit a few
       px apart most of the shot while their averages are far apart.
     - the 60 px is scaled by plate width / 1920, so one slider value looks the
       same on HD, 4K and 6K.
   The surviving COUNT floats with the footage. Dense texture clears the spacing
   test in more places, so it exports more. There is no fixed target number.

23. OUTPUT CEILING (max_output_tracks = 600)                               [ON]
   A safety ceiling, not a target. Best-scoring tracks are taken first, so the
   cap keeps the good ones. If only 200 clear the quality bar, 200 ship.

24. START-TIME SPACING (spread_max_starts_per_window = 0)                 [OFF]
   Would cap how many tracks are allowed to START within the same short window,
   so track births are spread through the shot as well as across the frame.
   Off -- staggered seeding already largely solved the bulk-arrival problem.


================================================================================
SECTION E -- INSIDE THE PATTERN REFINE (per frame, per segment)
(app/pattern_refine.py). This is where a track is polished to sub-pixel and
where most per-frame rejections happen.
================================================================================

Background: the moving-tile stage runs first and only MOVES positions -- it
never drops a track. Then pattern refine cuts a small image patch at the point's
first good frame (the "anchor") and matches it into every other frame at full
plate resolution, the way 3DE does.

25. LOCK-LOST BAR (refine_ncc_lost = 0.60)                                 [ON]
   If the best match in a frame correlates below 0.60 with the pattern, the lock
   is considered lost and the track is cut there (or handed to re-acquisition,
   filter 30). Auto-tune moves it: 0.48 on a grainy plate (grain lowers every
   score, so a fixed bar throws away good matches), 0.68 on a clean one.

26. HYSTERESIS HOLD BAR (refine_ncc_hold = 0.45)                           [ON]
   A single hard threshold means a correlation hovering right at 0.60 drops the
   point for a frame here and there, and the track looks like it is flickering.
   So: it takes 0.60 to declare a lock, but only 0.45 to KEEP one. A frame held
   this way is never allowed to re-grab the pattern.

27. RIVAL-PEAK / AMBIGUITY REJECT (match_ambiguity_ratio = 0.90)           [ON]
   Repetitive detail -- rivets, bolts, window grids, tiles -- gives the matcher
   several near-equal answers. Taking the single best silently snapped the point
   to the identical feature NEXT DOOR, at high correlation, so nothing
   downstream noticed. Now, if a second peak scores 90% or more of the winner,
   the frame is refused outright. "I don't know" beats a confident wrong answer.

28. RE-REFERENCE TRIGGER (refine_ncc_reref = 0.68)                         [ON]
   As the shot goes on, lighting and perspective change and the original patch
   stops matching. Below 0.68 (but still above the lost bar) the bot re-grabs a
   fresh patch. This bar used to be 0.85, which meant re-grabbing on nearly
   every frame -- that quietly turns a pattern tracker into a frame-to-frame one,
   which random-walks off the feature. Re-referencing is now the exception.

29. DRIFT GUARD (refine_drift_floor = 0.55, re-check every 24 frames)      [ON]
   A re-grabbed patch is only accepted if it STILL correlates 0.55 or better
   with the ORIGINAL anchor patch. Otherwise re-referencing has nothing tying it
   to where it started and the point wanders off over a long shot. The anchor is
   also re-checked every 24 frames regardless.

30. RE-ACQUISITION AFTER A GAP (max gap 24 frames, bar 0.75, search 50%)   [ON]
   A lost lock used to end that side of the track -- which is how points were
   lost to occluders SAM3 never masked (a pole, a prop, a hand). Now the bot
   keeps stepping through the gap and tries to re-find the ORIGINAL anchor
   patch. Where to look is predicted from nearby surviving tracks. It demands a
   higher bar than mid-track (0.75, not 0.60) and searches only HALF the normal
   radius, so a rival feature further away cannot win the re-grab. Gives up
   after 24 frames.

31. SPLIT UNVERIFIED SEGMENTS (split_unverified_segments)                  [ON]
   If the segment after a gap does NOT pass the anchor check, it is emitted as
   a SEPARATE track rather than welded onto the old ID. Welding two different
   features together is invisible until the camera solve fails, so on any doubt
   it splits.

32. END EXTENSION, STOPS AT FIRST FAILURE (refine_extend_max = 48 frames)  [ON]
   NOT A DELETE -- AN EXTENSION, with a filter built in. A track starts where a
   seed happened to enter and stops where its pass ran out; neither says
   anything about the feature, usually still visible either side. So the bot
   walks outward from the OUTER ends, matching the original anchor at the
   position the track's own speed predicts, demanding the strict 0.75 bar, and
   verifying each frame backwards. It STOPS at the first failed frame -- because
   skipping a bad frame is exactly how a point walks through an occluder and
   re-attaches on the far side. It never walks into an internal gap; that gap is
   an occlusion and filter 30 is what crosses it.

33. FORWARD-BACKWARD CONSISTENCY (refine_fb_max_px = 1.5 px)               [ON]
   Refine the segment, then re-run it backwards. A good point returns to where
   it started. If the MEDIAN return error exceeds 1.5 px the whole segment is
   rejected; if only a contiguous TAIL fails, just that tail is trimmed (a
   perforated track full of holes is useless to 3DE, so this never punches
   single frames out of the middle). Self-referential, so parallax is safe.
   Roughly doubles refine time.

34. REFINED-TRACK MINIMUM LENGTH (refine_min_len = 8 frames)               [ON]
   After all of the above trimming, a refined piece shorter than 8 frames is
   dropped.

35. 1-D FEATURE DROP (segment reason "edge")                               [ON]
   If the refiner finds a segment's correlation surface is a RIDGE rather than a
   peak, the point is on a line, not a corner (same problem as filter 5, caught
   later at full resolution). Those points are dropped rather than kept in their
   raw state -- raw they would be both jittery and still sliding.

36. ZERO-VARIANCE PATCH REJECT                                             [ON]
   A patch of flat sky or a blown-out highlight has no variation, and the
   correlation maths divides by its standard deviation. Rejected outright rather
   than producing a meaningless number.


================================================================================
SECTION F -- POST-REFINE GATES (order is deliberate)
(app/tracker_core.py lines ~1990-2025, implemented in app/track_filter.py)
================================================================================

37. SEED IDENTITY GATE (min_seed_identity = 0.25)                          [ON]
   Compares the track's patch at its FIRST frame with its patch at its LAST
   frame. This is the ONLY test that catches a point which slid SMOOTHLY off its
   feature over a whole shot. Such a track has a sharp correlation peak every
   frame (so certainty passes it), runs the whole shot unbroken (so the score
   ranks it TOP), and never jitters (so wobble sees nothing).
   Measured on a real plate: the drifter scored -0.055 while every other
   exported track scored 0.50 to 0.99. So this is an outlier catch, not a
   ranking. It is an ABSOLUTE bar, because "is this still the same patch" means
   the same thing on every plate.
   Safety rail: if more than half the export fails it, the PLATE changed
   (a lighting cut, a whip pan) rather than every track drifting -- so it is not
   applied at all.
   Runs FIRST and on the full set, deliberately: a drifter must also leave the
   backfill pool (filter 39), or it simply comes back through that door.

38. CERTAINTY GATE -- ABSOLUTE FLOOR (min_track_certainty, auto-tuned)     [ON]
   Certainty = how SHARP the correlation peak was, i.e. how confidently the
   point was localised. It can only be known after refine, which is why this
   gate cannot live with the others. It is the one signal that catches a
   defocused point, because such a point's motion statistics -- long, smooth, no
   jumps -- are excellent and fool every other test.
   The floor is set by the auto-tune from how much of the FRAME is in focus:
     - under 25% of frame sharp -> 0.35 (nearly every seed lands on soft ground)
     - soft, or under 50% sharp -> 0.25
     - otherwise               -> 0.12

38b. CERTAINTY GATE -- RELATIVE BAR (certainty_rel = 0.80)              [COND]
   An absolute certainty number cannot travel between plates -- it depends on
   contrast, grain and lens, so 0.5 is excellent on one shot and poor on
   another. So each track is also compared to its OWN shot's best.
   Critically, this only fires when the shot's numbers show TWO POPULATIONS with
   a clean gap between them (the sharp-subject / defocused-background case).
   On a single smeared-out population it is switched off, because slicing an
   arbitrary percentile off a continuum cut 13 of 23 tracks on a bench shot
   whose true error was 0.06 px on every one of them.
   Where a real gap exists the bot cuts AT THE GAP rather than at a percentile,
   because on a defocused background the GOOD tracks are the minority and a
   percentile bar lands inside the bad cluster.

38c. CERTAINTY CUT CAP (certainty_max_cut = 0.6)                           [ON]
   If the gate wants to remove more than 60% of the tracks AND there is no clean
   gap in the numbers, it is measuring badly rather than finding something --
   so the cut is capped and the best 40% are kept. If there IS a clean gap, the
   cut stands at any size, because then it is discriminating, not arbitrary.

38d. UNMEASURED = NOT JUDGED                                               [ON]
   A track with no certainty number (refine could not anchor it at all) is NOT
   a track with bad certainty. Those are excluded from the distribution and
   never dropped by it. Counting them as zero once both condemned them AND
   manufactured a fake gap wide enough to look like two populations, which
   dropped 23 accurate tracks on a bench shot.

39. BACKFILL TO FLOOR (min_export_tracks = 40)                             [ON]
   THE ONLY ANTI-FILTER. A handful of tracks with no explanation is not a usable
   delivery. If the export has fallen below 40 tracks, it is topped back up from
   the best rejects, and those additions are FLAGGED as weak in the track report
   so the artist knows which ones to look at.
   The top-up is ranked by wobble, not by score, because on real footage wobble
   is the only measure here that predicts true error (+0.573 correlation), while
   score is actively misleading (+0.291 -- it ranks drifters as the best tracks).

40. WOBBLE GATE (wobble_rel = 0.0)                                        [OFF]
   Would drop any track deviating from its own smooth path by more than 1.5x the
   shot's own median. It runs LAST and on the FINAL set, so it can see what the
   top-up just added -- a filter that sees only part of the export cannot remove
   a track that came in through another door.
   Off by default: measured on real footage it cut 14 of 29 tracks and left the
   worst one (20.5 px) untouched, because that track drifts SMOOTHLY and wobble
   only measures deviation from a track's own smooth path. Kept as a slider for
   shots where visible jitter is the actual complaint.

41. DEFRAGMENT (max 2 holes, each >= 3 frames, each run >= 8 frames)       [ON]
   Occlusion continuity (filter 8) deliberately lets a track survive a crossing
   by carrying a hole. One or two long holes is exactly that, and worth keeping
   under one ID.
   MANY SHORT holes is a different animal: not an occluded track but a marginal
   one that kept losing and regaining lock -- and it blinks on and off in the
   3DE viewport like an LED. So a track may carry at most 2 holes, each at least
   3 frames long (i.e. plausibly something really crossing), and every visible
   stretch must be at least 8 frames. Anything else is CUT into its continuous
   runs, and runs too short to use are dropped. A solid track is never touched
   here whatever its length -- length is the quality gate's job.

42. GEOMETRIC + APERTURE REPORTS                                  [REPORT ONLY]
   Two measurements that are written to the track report and the log but NEVER
   used to drop anything:
     - Epipolar residual: how well each track agrees with the shot's overall
       camera geometry. Deliberately never gated, because a point on a real
       moving object is off-geometry AND correct -- gating on this would delete
       exactly the object tracks the bot was asked for.
     - Aperture report: says out loud when most of what got tracked can only
       slide along an edge. On a plate whose only structure IS edges there is
       nothing better to offer, so the batch publishes and flags for review
       rather than returning an empty shot.

ALSO OFF: PER-TRACK POLICY (per_track_policy)                             [OFF]
   Not a filter itself -- it would give every track its OWN settings (pattern
   box size, motion model, NCC bars) based on what its seed is sitting on
   (corner / blob / edge / dense) instead of one setting for the whole shot.
   Turned on during development, then turned back off when measured on a real
   soft plate: the classifier judges cornerness against the FRAME'S OWN
   percentiles, so on a plate where everything is soft it still labels the top
   quartile "corner" and SHRINKS their pattern box -- the opposite of what a
   soft feature needs. It fought the shot-level auto-tune and lost, on exactly
   the shot type it was enabled to help.


================================================================================
SECTION G -- THE SYNTHEYES BACKEND (the default engine)
(app/syntheyes_engine.py, _apply_sam3_postfilter)
================================================================================

SynthEyes has its own tracker and its own notion of a good track. Grading it
with measurements derived from the TAPNext path mixed two policies with no
number to justify either, so that was deliberately stopped. Its output is
published as tracked, with only two filters:

S1. SAM3 MASK POST-FILTER
   Every exported point is sampled against that frame's mask. White (>=127) is
   background and is KEPT; black is a mover and is DROPPED. A point whose frame
   has no matching mask is kept ungated (and the count of those is logged
   separately, so "the mask did nothing" and "the mask dropped everything" do
   not look alike -- a plate numbered from 1001 against 120 masks lines up with
   nothing).

S2. MINIMUM TRACK LENGTH (5 points)
   After gating, a track with fewer than 5 surviving points is dropped.

S3. ALL-GATED-AWAY SAFETY
   If the gating wipes out every track, the shot is FAILED and the export file
   removed, rather than overwriting a good ungated export with an empty one
   (which would then publish over a previously good delivery). The masks are
   probably inverted or misaligned.

Note: a full score-gate-spread-cap chain for finished tracks exists in
app/track_filter.py (filter_tracks) but has had no caller since 2026-08-11. It
is kept intact so reinstating it is a decision, not a rewrite.


================================================================================
SECTION H -- FILTERS OUTSIDE THE TRACKING STAGE
================================================================================

ANALYZE STAGE
   Qwen describes each shot; deterministic term lists in the heuristics module
   filter those descriptions into SAM3 include/exclude prompts. Client
   requirements (the requirements file and anything typed in the UI) are merged
   in and win.

MASK STAGE
   - Mask dilation (10 px): grows the exclude region at MASK time, because SAM3
     edges are soft and a matte that stops short of the silhouette leaves a halo.
   - Reuse existing masks: if a shot already has masks, SAM3 is skipped.
   - Motion backstop: OFF by default. It would mask anything moving
     independently of the camera as a fourth safety net -- but that includes
     objects the artist deliberately removed from the exclude list, so it
     overrides a decision made on purpose. Opt in per shot.

FRAME RANGE
   frame_start / frame_end (1-based, inclusive, 0 = unset) slice the clip before
   anything runs. Exported frame numbers stay aligned to the original shot.

QUICK SUMMARY -- WHAT IS OFF BY DEFAULT
================================================================================
   5b  adaptive anisotropy floor       -- needs measuring
   17  jump / jitter hard limits       -- blunt, per-shot guesswork
   18  path smoothing                  -- hides error rather than fixing it
   20  pass fusion                     -- measured on two shots and rejected
   21b span-fraction length gate       -- absolute frame count used instead
   24  start-time spacing              -- staggered seeding solved it
   40  wobble gate                     -- cut 14/29 and missed the real drifter
   --  per-track policy                -- classifier is relative, fails on soft
                                          plates

WHAT CATCHES WHAT (the short version)
   Point on an edge, slides along it ......... 5, 35
   Point on a mover / occluded ............... 3, 8, 11, S1
   Point that teleports ...................... 14, 21c
   Point that snapped to an identical rival ... 27, 30
   Point that is soft / badly localised ...... 38
   Point that slid SMOOTHLY off its feature ... 37 (nothing else sees it)
   Point that blinks on and off .............. 9, 41
   Too many points, clumped .................. 22, 23
   Too few points ............................ 21d, 38c, 39 (all push back)
